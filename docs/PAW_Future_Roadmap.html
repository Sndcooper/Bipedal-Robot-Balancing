🐾 PAW — Analysis, Novelty & Research Roadmap

A wheel-legged self-balancing robot: STM32F103 Blue Pill, 4× Dynamixel AX-12+ driving two 5-bar legs, two JGA12V encoder motors, MPU6050, 3DR telemetry, and FlySky iBUS. This page covers where the rcbfw firmware and GUI stand today, what the logs show, how to fix the servo and jerkiness bottlenecks, and which research directions could become papers.

⚖️ Balance

  • Derivative on measurement (-gyroRate), so setpoint steps cause no D kick.
  • The integrator state is clamped to 64 PWM, not just the output.
  • Tilt cutoff at 25° latches until re-armed.

🔁 Velocity cascade

  • A drive command becomes a lean angle, never a PWM. There is a single authority over the setpoint.
  • Trim integrator learns only when standing (|v err| < 250 c/s, no stick input).
  • The velocity setpoint is slew-limited, so releasing the stick decelerates instead of lurching.

🎮 RC layer

  • IBUSBM_NOTIMER: no TIM1 ISR. A cooperative drain with a 400 µs budget, polled every 10 ms.
  • Per-channel centre and switch idle state captured at boot, plus a 3-poll debounce.
  • Arm interlock, 500 ms link-loss failsafe, and Ch7 always works as a kill switch.

🦿 Legs

  • 2-circle IK on the MCU, calibrated 818/441 counts, mirrored leg 2.
  • Rule of one Serial2 transaction per tick: move, settings, or hold/poll.
  • Settings are pushed one servo per tick via a dirty-mask. The health poll is non-blocking and reads the ERROR byte.
  • After a limp, re-grip reads present position and re-seeds the foot with FK, so the legs don't snap.

What's genuinely novel here and what is good engineering rather than novelty

Idea in the codeAssessment
A 5-bar parallel leg on a wheel, driven by ~$45 serial-bus servos, with a variable-height wheeled inverted pendulum balanced on a 72 MHz Cortex-M3 with no FPU. publishable platform angle Comparable robots (Ascento, Upkie, Diablo, Tron1) use BLDC or QDD actuators that cost 10–50× more. Being this cheap and low-bandwidth is the research hook.
Learn-band-gated trim integrator: the outer I-term learns the CoM offset only near steady state and freezes while driving or braking. nice, practical A form of conditional integration. It is well-motivated by the logs but known in the anti-windup literature.
Rule of one bus transaction per tick on a half-duplex bus built from a 10 kΩ resistor, with byte-counted echo rejection. solid real-time design Engineering, not a research contribution. The divergence below shows it is easy to break.
Boot-captured switch idle state and debounce: a two-position switch has no neutral, so its resting position is defined as idle. clever safety detail Worth documenting and not novel. The logs showed Ch8 resting 109 µs off-centre, which is what drove the design.
Cooperative, budgeted iBUS drain instead of the library's 1 ms ISR that contains delayMicroseconds(100). correct call The ISR in the library was verified (IBusBM.cpp:213).
Log-evidence-driven tuning culture: every constant in main.cpp cites the session log that justified it. strong basis for a dataset Structured, these logs become a real-robot dataset for learning-based papers.

Findings that should change the code ordered by impact

high

1 · The inner-loop Ki acts as a relay

With Ki = 650 the integral saturates at ±0.0985 (64 PWM) in about half of all armed frames and flips between rails about 0.6 times per second. That is a ±64 PWM square wave feeding the motors, which matches the slow 4–6 s wobble and the idle drift.

Fix: drop the inner-loop Ki to about 100–250, or remove it and let the outer trim loop own the offset (PD inner, PI outer is the textbook split). Add a small leak.

high

2 · Two "main" firmwares have diverged

rcbfw was forked from rc_balance_fusion_wireless at commit 16ef5ba. After the fork, rbfw gained the D-term low-pass, the SAT telemetry field and the crouchDirty bus fix. rcbfw gained the velocity slew, drive expo, trim learn band and the poll flush fix. Neither variant has all of them.

Fix: merge both into one firmware. The rcbfw RC-crouch path still calls ax12SyncWriteGoals() from readRC(), which is a second bus transaction per tick. That is exactly the bug rbfw documented as killing the bus.

high

3 · Whole-bus AX-12 dropouts

In 6 sessions all four servos stopped replying within about 4 s of arming (the 1 s stagger is just the SRV report round-robin). A single bad servo would not do this. The likely causes are bus desync or a supply sag under motor current, not individual servo faults.

Fix: the merged arbiter, a native single-wire half-duplex USART, logging of servo voltage (addr 42), and a separate servo supply or bulk capacitance.

medium

4 · IMU is under-configured

  • Wire.setClock() is never called, so I²C runs at 100 kHz. That costs about 1.3–1.5 ms, or 13–15% of every tick (the docs claim 400 kHz).
  • The MPU6050 DLPF (reg 0x1A) is never set, so the gyro runs at about 256 Hz bandwidth and motor/servo vibration goes straight into the D-term.
  • There is no gyro bias calibration; C only zeroes the accelerometer.
  • rcbfw has no D-term filter (rbfw has DF).
medium

5 · Coarse velocity measurement

Encoders are decoded on RISING edges of channel A only (1×). At 100 Hz one count is 100 c/s of quantisation, which is why the EMA is so heavy (≈2.6 Hz corner, not the "15 Hz" the skill doc claims).

Fix: use CHANGE interrupts on A and B for 4× decoding, or TIM3 encoder mode on PA6/PA7. Then estimate velocity from time between edges or with a small Kalman filter.

medium

6 · Actuation non-idealities

The L298N (~2 V bipolar drop) runs at STM32duino's default ~1 kHz analogWrite with no deadband compensation. The median |PID_OUT| is 72 PWM, so a real share of the command range goes into the driver's deadzone.

Fix: use a TB6612FNG or DRV8871-class driver, 10–20 kHz PWM, and a measured friction and deadband feed-forward.

low

7 · Observability

At 10 Hz, telemetry cannot see jitter. The PITCH line is about 350 B.

Fix: a compact binary frame (about 24 B) at 50 Hz, plus an on-board RAM ring buffer that is dumped after a cutoff (a "black box").

low

8 · No persistence

A brownout reset silently restores the compiled gains while the GUI keeps showing stale sliders.

Fix: store gains and calibration in flash (EEPROM emulation), and send a boot-count and reset-reason line.

Servo bottleneck & jerkiness the AX-12 is the weak link, so work around it

AX-12+: 1 Mbps half-duplex, 0.29° resolution, an internal P-only controller shaped by compliance margin and slope, 1.5 N·m stall torque, and no velocity profile of its own unless you set Moving Speed.

🧵 Synchronised velocity profiling

For each move, SYNC_WRITE goal and Moving Speed (addr 30–33, 4 bytes per servo) with each servo's speed proportional to its travel. All four joints then arrive together and the servo's own profile does the smoothing, instead of sending 10 Hz goal steps.

📈 Minimum-jerk trajectories

Replace the cubic smoothstep (acceleration jumps at start and end) with the quintic 10s³−15s⁴+6s⁵, which has zero velocity and zero acceleration at both ends. Stream at 50 Hz during moves. At 1 Mbps a 20-byte SYNC_WRITE is about 0.2 ms.

🎚️ Setpoint filter on RC crouch

Pass the rate-integrated crouch target through a critically damped second-order filter (ωn about 8 rad/s) before IK. You get smooth arrival with no restart problem, which is the fix the code comments already point to.

🧲 Compliance gain scheduling

Posing: slope 4, margin 1 (stiff). Balancing on the ground: slope 32–64, which makes the legs act as passive suspension that soaks up wheel impacts before they reach the IMU. Switch automatically when the robot arms. This is the "Phase C" item from the old roadmap.

🔌 Native half-duplex UART

The STM32 USART supports single-wire half-duplex (HDSEL). In STM32duino this is HardwareSerial Serial2(PA2) with a single pin. It removes the 10 kΩ hack and the echo accounting. A 74LVC2G241 tri-state buffer is the other option. Either way the echo-drain parser needs updating to match.

📏 Feed-forward pitch compensation

Crouching moves the CoM and changes the pendulum length. Use the IK foot positions to feed forward a targetAngle correction and schedule Kp/Kd with body height (a simple lookup table). Today a crouch shows up as a disturbance the PID has to reject.

⚡ Power isolation

Put the servo rail on its own regulator or LiPo tap with 1000 µF or more of bulk capacitance, and log AX-12 voltage (addr 42). Motor stalls sagging the shared rail is the most likely cause of the whole-bus dropouts and resets.

🔁 Upgrade path

For dynamic leg use (active suspension, jumping) the AX-12 is too slow and weak. Dynamixel XL430 or XC330 (Protocol 2.0, current and velocity modes, 4 Mbps) are a drop-in class upgrade. Small QDD BLDC actuators are the long-term option.

Features that would lift performance

control

LQR / state-feedback balance

The state is [pitch, pitch-rate, wheel position, wheel velocity]. Identify the model from logs, compute a gain K per leg height, and interpolate between them. It replaces three hand-tuned loops with one principled controller and gives a clean baseline for papers.

estimation

Kalman / Mahony pitch plus gyro-bias estimation

Fuse accelerometer, gyro and wheel odometry (the base-acceleration term corrupts the accelerometer when driving). Estimate gyro bias online, and detect slip from the disagreement between encoder and IMU.

legs

Roll balance and leaning into turns

Differential leg height from IMU roll lets the robot level itself on slopes and lean into turns for higher yaw rates without tipping, as Ascento does. The hardware already allows this.

legs

Step-up / curb climb

Crouch, drive the wheel against the edge, extend. This is feasible with AX-12s at low speed and makes a strong demo video.

autonomy

Heading hold and odometry

Integrate yaw from the encoder difference, fuse it with gyro Z, and close a heading loop (the code notes this is missing). Add a waypoint mode from the GUI.

tooling

MuJoCo digital twin

Build an MJCF model of PAW with the real mass numbers from Robot_Specification.md, AX-12 latency and quantisation, and L298N deadband. It enables sim-to-real, controller regression tests, and RL.

tooling

Safe auto-tuner (Bayesian optimisation)

Revive legacy/testing/autotuner against the current protocol. Use SafeOpt or a constrained BO over (Kp, Kd, Kp_vel, Ki_trim) with the cutoff as the safety constraint.

compute

Companion computer

Add an ESP32-S3 or Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W over UART to run learned policies or vision (line following, person following), while the Blue Pill stays the hard-real-time safety layer.

Paper topics ICRA · IROS · CoRL · RSS · Humanoids · RA-L

No topic guarantees acceptance. What gets accepted at these venues is: (1) a clear claim, (2) comparison against strong baselines (LQR, a tuned PID, prior work), (3) real-hardware experiments with statistics over many trials, and (4) ideally open-sourced code, CAD and data. The low-cost, low-bandwidth nature of PAW is the angle that sets it apart, so lean into it. Check each venue's call for papers for current deadlines and page limits.
CoRL · ICRA · RA-L

A · Actuator-aware sim-to-real RL for wheel-legged robots with cheap serial-bus servos

Claim: modelling bus latency, 0.29° quantisation, compliance-slope dynamics and driver deadband (an "actuator network", as in Hwangbo et al. 2019) is what makes an RL policy transfer on a $300 robot.

Experiments: policy with and without the actuator model; against LQR and PID on push recovery, velocity tracking and crouch-while-balancing.

ICRA · IROS

B · Residual RL on top of a cascaded PID for safe on-robot learning

Claim: a small residual policy (running on a companion MCU) on top of the existing cascade learns on hardware within minutes without falling, while the PID guarantees a stable fallback.

Experiments: falls per training hour, disturbance rejection before and after, comparison with learning from scratch in simulation.

IROS · Humanoids · RA-L

C · Height-scheduled control of a variable-height wheeled inverted pendulum with 5-bar legs

Claim: LPV or gain-scheduled LQR over leg height, with CoM feed-forward from IK, keeps performance uniform across a 40 mm crouch range where a fixed-gain controller degrades.

Experiments: pitch RMS and recovery time at 5 heights, during dynamic crouching, and on stairs or curbs.

ICRA · IROS

D · Learned variable impedance for position-controlled servos

Claim: scheduling AX-12 compliance margin and slope online (learned, or from a disturbance estimator) gives passive suspension that measurably reduces pitch disturbance from terrain, without torque-controlled actuators.

Experiments: bumps, drops and gravel. Compare stiff, soft, and learned scheduling; measure IMU vibration and pitch spikes.

ICRA · CASE · RA-L

E · Safe Bayesian optimisation of balance gains from minutes of hardware data

Claim: SafeOpt or constrained BO with the tilt-cutoff as a safety constraint finds better gains than expert hand-tuning within N trials and never trips the cutoff.

Asset: the existing log pipeline and auto-backup already produce the data.

IROS · RA-L · Sensors

F · Learned proprioceptive state estimation on a microcontroller

Claim: a tiny network or Kalman filter hybrid (running on the MCU through TinyML) fusing IMU, wheel encoders and servo present-position/load estimates pitch, CoM shift and wheel slip better than a complementary filter.

Experiments: motion-capture or camera ground truth; accuracy against compute cost on a Cortex-M.

ICRA · HardwareX · IEEE RAM

G · PAW: an open, sub-$300 wheel-legged research platform

Claim: a reproducible platform with CAD, firmware, a MuJoCo twin and a dataset, benchmarked against published wheel-legged robots on cost, bandwidth and capabilities. Platform papers get cited if others can actually build the robot.

CoRL · ICRA (LLM track)

H · Language-to-skill on a low-cost wheel-legged robot

Claim: an LLM or VLM on the companion computer composes parametrised skills (drive, crouch, lean, climb) with a safety monitor on the MCU. The contribution must be in the safety-constrained grounding, not the demo itself.

Strongest bets for a first paper: C (height-scheduled control) and E (safe BO) are achievable with the current hardware and a MuJoCo model. A and B have the most impact but need the simulation twin and better telemetry first.

Roadmap

Phase 0 — Consolidate (week 1)

Merge rcbfw and rbfw. Apply the IMU, Ki, encoder and PWM quick wins. Add flash-persisted parameters. Add a CI build (pio run) for the main firmware. Archive the superseded variants under legacy/.

Phase 1 — Smooth & observable (weeks 2–3)

Minimum-jerk trajectories, synchronised Moving Speed, crouch setpoint filter, compliance scheduling. Binary 50 Hz telemetry and black box. Servo power isolation. Metric: zero bus dropouts, crouch without a pitch spike above 2°.

Phase 2 — Model-based (weeks 4–6)

System identification from logs, then a MuJoCo twin, then LQR per height with CoM feed-forward. Add a Kalman estimator and heading hold. Metric: pitch σ below 0.8° free-standing, push recovery with less than 10 cm drift.

Phase 3 — Legs as actuators (weeks 7–9)

Roll balance, leaning into turns, step-up climbing. Consider the XL430 upgrade.

Phase 4 — Learning & papers (weeks 10+)

Safe BO tuner (paper E), height-scheduled control (paper C), then a companion computer with residual or sim-to-real RL (papers A and B). Open-source release (paper G).
